Tokio enters here, because the design needs it. One task owns the board. Inputs arrive on a bounded channel, the view leaves on a watch, and effects go to a runner that owns every watcher. None of it may stall an agent: the forwarder gives up after 500 ms, and it has to find the daemon answering.

Desired behaviour

  • An input that changes the board publishes a new view, and subscribers wake to see the new shown phases.
  • An input that changes nothing visible, such as a duplicate prompt, wakes nobody.
  • Every effect except publish reaches the runner, in order. Publish itself never does. A runner that has gone away is ignored, not a panic.
  • With nothing sent, an inferred escalation still shows at its deadline, and a quiet turn still ends after ten minutes. The core sleeps until the board's own next deadline, recomputed after every input.
  • When every sender is dropped, the loop drains what's queued, ends, and hands back the board.
  • All times come from tokio's clock, so the tests pause it and nothing really waits.
